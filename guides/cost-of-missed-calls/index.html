What missed calls really cost your business

A simple way to put a dollar figure on the calls you don't pick up, and what to do about them.

By the King Connect team · Updated November 24, 2026

Every small business owner around Cedar Creek Lake knows the feeling: you're under a sink, on a roof or behind the chair, the phone buzzes, and by the time you call back the customer has hired someone else. One missed call doesn't feel like much. Over a month, it adds up to real money.

The simple math

You only need three numbers to estimate what missed calls cost you:

  1. New-customer calls you miss in a week. Not calls from your mom or a supplier, just people who might hire you.
  2. How many of those would have hired you. If 4 out of 10 people who reach you usually book, use 40%.
  3. What an average job is worth. Use a typical ticket, not your biggest job of the year.

Multiply them together for a weekly number, then multiply by about 4.3 for a month.

Example: 6 missed calls a week × 40% who would have booked × $400 average job = $960 a week. That's about $4,160 a month, or almost $50,000 a year. Your numbers will be different, which is why it's worth running them.

Why missed calls hurt more than they look

The lost job is only part of it. A customer who reaches someone else usually becomes that company's repeat customer too. They save the number, they call them next spring, and they tell their neighbor who they used. When you miss the first call, you can lose every call after it.

Timing makes it worse. The calls you miss tend to come at your busiest moments: the first cold snap, the first 100-degree weekend, the Saturday before a holiday at the lake. Those are the callers who need help right now and won't wait.

How to find out how many calls you're missing

  • Open your phone's recent calls and count the missed ones from numbers you don't recognize over a normal week.
  • Check how many of those left a voicemail. The ones that didn't are usually the ones who called someone else.
  • If you have a business line through a phone company or app, look for a call log or report that shows unanswered calls.

What you can do about it

  • Call back fast. Minutes matter more than hours. If you can check your phone between jobs, return calls before you start the next one.
  • Text back automatically. A missed-call text-back system sends a text from your business number seconds after you miss a call, asking how you can help. The customer knows you got the call and usually waits for you instead of dialing the next name. Read how missed-call text back works.
  • Put your hours and a way to text you on your Google profile. People who can't reach you by phone will often message instead.

Common questions

How many calls does a small business miss?

It varies a lot by trade. One-person shops that work with their hands, like HVAC techs, plumbers and lawn crews, tend to miss the most because they can't answer mid-job. The easiest way to know is to look at your phone's recent calls for a normal week and count the ones you didn't pick up.

Do people leave a voicemail if I miss their call?

Many don't. A lot of callers hang up and try the next business instead, especially when something is broken and they need help today. That's why a fast text back matters more than a voicemail greeting.

What's the fastest way to stop losing missed calls?

Reply to every missed caller within a minute or two, even if it's just a text saying you'll call back. A missed-call text-back system does this automatically from your business number.
