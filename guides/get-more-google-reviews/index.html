How to get more Google reviews

Seven habits that get local businesses steady, honest reviews, without breaking Google's rules.

By the King Connect team · Updated November 24, 2026

When someone around Gun Barrel City or Mabank searches for a plumber, a salon or a boat mechanic, Google shows them a map with a few businesses and their star ratings. The businesses with more recent, good reviews get more of those calls. The good news is that getting reviews is mostly a habit, not a secret.

1. Ask every customer, every time

Most happy customers will leave a review if you ask. Most won't think to do it on their own. The businesses with lots of reviews aren't necessarily better; they just ask consistently.

2. Make it one tap

Don't tell people to "find us on Google." Send them your direct review link. You can get it from your Google Business Profile (look for "Ask for reviews" or "Get more reviews"). Text it right after the job:

"Thanks for choosing us today! If you have 30 seconds, a quick Google review really helps a small local business: [your link]"

3. Ask while the job is fresh

The same day is best. A week later, the customer has moved on and the moment is gone. If you finish jobs on the go, a text that goes out automatically when you mark a job done takes the remembering off your plate.

4. Ask everyone, not just the happy ones

It's tempting to only ask customers you're sure will leave five stars, or to send unhappy customers to a private feedback form instead. Google calls that "review gating" and doesn't allow it. Ask everyone the same way. If something went wrong, the better move is to call that customer and make it right.

5. Don't pay or trade for reviews

Discounts, gift cards or freebies in exchange for reviews break Google's rules and can get reviews removed. Buying fake reviews is worse and can get a profile suspended.

6. Reply to every review

Thank people by name and mention the job if you can. For a bad review, stay calm, apologize for the experience, and offer to fix it offline. Future customers read your replies as much as the reviews themselves.

7. Keep it steady

A few new reviews every month beats twenty in one week and nothing after. A steady trickle tells Google and customers that you're busy and still doing good work.

Reviews also help you show up higher on Google Maps for searches around the lake.

Common questions

Is it against Google's rules to ask for reviews?

No. Google allows businesses to ask customers for reviews. What it doesn't allow is paying for reviews, offering discounts or gifts in exchange, or only asking happy customers while steering unhappy ones away.

When is the best time to ask for a review?

Right after the job is done and the customer is happy, ideally the same day. A text with a direct link works well because customers can leave the review on their phone in a minute.

Should I reply to every Google review?

Yes. Short, personal replies to good reviews show you're active, and a calm, helpful reply to a bad review shows future customers how you handle problems.
