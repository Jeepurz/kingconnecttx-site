How to show up on Google Maps around the lake

The free steps that get a local business into the map results when people nearby search for what you do.

By the King Connect team · Updated November 24, 2026

When someone in Seven Points searches "AC repair near me" or "hair salon Gun Barrel City," Google shows a map with three businesses before anything else. Getting into that map is one of the best free ways for a local business to get calls. Here's what actually moves the needle.

1. Claim and verify your Google Business Profile

Everything starts here. Go to business.google.com, claim or create your business, and finish verification. Google usually asks for a short video showing your location, your business name and proof you run it. An unverified profile won't show up properly on Maps.

2. Fill out every part of it

  • Category: pick the most specific primary category that fits (for example, "HVAC contractor," not just "Contractor"), then add a few secondary ones.
  • Service area: list the towns you actually serve, like Mabank, Gun Barrel City, Seven Points, Tool, Kemp, Eustace, Malakoff and Athens.
  • Hours, phone and website: keep them accurate, including holiday hours.
  • Services and description: describe what you do in plain words customers would search for.

3. Keep your name, address and phone the same everywhere

Your business name and phone number should match exactly on Google, Facebook, Yelp, Bing, Apple Maps and your website. Mismatches confuse Google about whether they're all the same business.

4. Get reviews, steadily

The number of reviews, how recent they are and your star rating all affect where you show up. See how to get more Google reviews without breaking Google's rules.

5. Post photos and updates

Add real photos of your work, your truck or your shop. Post an update every week or two: a finished job, a seasonal reminder, a holiday schedule. Active profiles look more trustworthy to customers.

6. Answer calls and messages fast

If Google sends you a caller and you don't pick up, that customer goes to the next listing on the map. Fast replies, even an automatic missed-call text back, keep those leads from slipping away.

7. Get listed in other places too

Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, Nextdoor, Facebook and local chamber directories all point back to your business. They help people find you on other maps and voice assistants, and they back up your Google listing.

Common questions

Do I need a website to show up on Google Maps?

No. A Google Business Profile can show up on Maps without a website. A website does help, though, because it gives Google more information about what you do and where.

Can I show up on Maps if I work from home?

Yes. Service businesses that go to their customers can list a service area instead of a street address, so your home address stays hidden.

How long does it take to show up?

After your profile is verified, it can appear within days, but ranking well for competitive searches usually takes a few months of steady reviews and updates.
